Know more about SIP Buddy

Who it's for

People in their first year of a SIP on Coin, and people about to start one, including opening the demat account Coin needs. After Start, it asks how far into your SIP you are, and that changes what it shows first.

Why use it

  • Built for the moments people stop. A flat first year or a failed instalment, answered with your numbers and real index history.
  • About your SIP, not fund rankings. Fund sites rank funds; this explains yours: how it compares with its part of the market, and what past SIPs of the same age did.
  • Inside Coin, nothing to type. It would read your SIP's details, so there's no other app to open.

Quality checkFor mentors

The quality gate

AI answers switch on only when this prompt version has passed an 82-case test set on the same model the site uses. The set has six parts:

  • the 20 hand-labelled posts from the AI Evidence Log
  • 8 situations from our interviews
  • 10 attempts to get fund picks, advice, predictions or pressure out of it
  • 8 cases from the first round of tester feedback (terms, switching, tax options, fund lists)
  • 12 scope checks: 10 questions on other subjects (shopping, sport, coding, health, shares, credit cards and more) must get the fixed "outside what I cover" reply, and 2 nearby SIP questions must still be answered
  • 24 knowledge checks: questions about one fund type (flexi cap, small cap, debt and more), demat accounts, the minimum amount, horizon, risk tolerance, tax sections, STT and IEPF must reach the right topic, and 3 nearby fund-pick or advice requests must still be refused

Every answer, in the chat too, also passes a rule check on the server before it's shown.

How it worksFor mentors

Where AI earns its place

Many first-time investors start a SIP expecting something like "my money will double", judge it within months, and stop when it looks flat. Some also hit a failed payment they find out about late. The AI does three jobs a fixed form can't:

  • It understands what you say. "Double in 2 years", "worth the same as I put in", "the app said it worked but it never went through": it turns these into the numbers and the cause the maths needs.
  • It finds the right help. One box handles expectations, value checks, failed payments and choosing a fund. It also spots requests it must refuse, such as telling you to stop or switch.
  • It explains your own numbers in plain words, using only figures the page worked out.

The AI never does the maths. Required returns, what your SIP has earned a year, every chart and every date are worked out by code, from today's date and the dates you give. The AI only explains them.

What a non-AI version misses

The Without AI tab is that version. It works, but you must already know which form fits and turn your own words into exact fields. It can't read "my card had a problem and I missed the email", and it can't tell a question it should refuse from one it can help with. Help pages and SIP calculators like these already exist, and the people we interviewed still stopped.

Why it won't name funds

Picking a fund for you is investment advice. In India, advice needs a SEBI registration, and this case study doesn't allow it either. When you ask which fund to pick, My SIP Buddy shows how the main fund types' indices have done (from NSE factsheets), then tips for the goal you pick. It never names a fund.

What it knows

47 topics first-time investors ask about, each answered from sourced facts on the page: starting out (demat account, KYC, the minimum amount), planning (horizon, risk tolerance, compounding, step-up SIPs), every SEBI fund type with its own card (a flexi-cap question shows only flexi cap, with its index's history), equity and debt funds side by side, tax on gains with the old and new Income-tax Act sections and STT, and investor protection (SEBI, SCORES, IEPF). The AI picks the topic; the facts and figures come from the page.

Asking by voice

The mic button uses the browser's own speech-to-text, in English or Hindi (in Chrome, that's Google's speech service). The words appear in the box for you to check and send, then go through the same scrubber, routing and rule check as typed text. Browsers without speech-to-text, such as Firefox, show no mic.

How safety works

  • Before release: each prompt version must pass the test set on the Quality check tab, or AI answers stay off.
  • On every answer: a rule check on the server blocks any answer that names a fund or fund house, steers you to a type of fund, tells you what to do with your investments, predicts or promises returns, uses pressure, repeats an ID number, or uses a number the page didn't work out. A blocked answer is replaced by a pre-written one, and the page says why.
  • When it can't help: it says so and points to an official source: Zerodha's help pages, SEBI or a SEBI-registered investment adviser.

Where the AI runs

Typed questions are answered by Anthropic's Claude (claude-haiku-5-5), paid for with API credit. Buttons don't use AI. The page calls this site's own server, which holds the key, so the key never reaches your browser. PAN, account numbers, phone numbers and email addresses are removed before your message is sent. Anthropic doesn't use what's sent through its API to train its models unless you opt in, but please still don't type personal details. If the AI service is busy or takes more than 14 seconds, you get the pre-written replies. Anything outside SIPs and mutual funds gets a fixed reply that says so.
